# The Naturalist Cogito: A Substrate Argument for Physical Existence

**David Harry Freed** | Independent Researcher | Olney, Maryland
**Date:** 2026-05-17

## Abstract

Descartes' Cogito establishes existence from the fact of thinking but leaves the nature of the thinking substrate unresolved, opening the door to idealism and dualism. This paper presents a corrective reformulation: the Naturalist Cogito. Beginning from the same apodictic starting point — reasoning is occurring — it derives not merely that something exists, but that something physical necessarily exists. The argument proceeds through three steps: reasoning is undeniable; reasoning requires a substrate with causal structure; such a substrate must be physical. The conclusion forecloses pure idealism and solipsism without assuming physicalism at the outset.

## 1. Motivation

Descartes' *Cogito ergo sum* — "I think, therefore I am" — is the most resilient argument in the Western philosophical tradition. Its power lies in its starting point: the act of doubting cannot itself be doubted, so some form of existence is guaranteed by the very attempt to deny it.

But the Cogito is deliberately agnostic about the nature of what exists. Descartes used it as a foundation for dualism, arguing that the thinking substance (*res cogitans*) is distinct from the extended substance (*res extensa*). This move is no longer tenable given what we now know about the physical requirements of information processing. The present argument closes the gap.

## 2. The Argument

**Premise 1 (Apodictic Starting Point):** Reasoning is occurring.

This is the same bedrock Descartes identified. It cannot be coherently denied — any denial is itself a reasoning act, which confirms the premise. We notate this simply as **R**.

**Premise 2 (Substrate Requirement):** Reasoning requires a substrate that supports causal structure.

Reasoning is not a static state but a process — an ordered sequence of state transitions, each dependent on the prior, unfolding in time. A process of this kind cannot occur in a causally inert or non-physical medium. There must be something that holds states, transitions between them, and preserves the causal order that makes inference possible. We notate this as **R → ∃M_R**: if reasoning occurs, a physical substrate for that reasoning exists.

This premise is not merely intuitive. It follows from the computational requirements of inference: memory, state encoding, sequential processing, and error correction all require physical implementation. No substrate, no sequence. No sequence, no reasoning.

**Premise 3 (Physical Grounding):** Any substrate that supports causal structure is physical.

A substrate capable of holding and transitioning states, maintaining causal order, and supporting temporal processes is, by definition, physical — it occupies space, consumes energy, and is subject to the laws of physics. Appeals to a purely mental or immaterial substrate are either circular (the mental is itself unexplained) or collapse back into physicalism under scrutiny. We notate this as **∃M_R → Physical**.

**Conclusion:** Something physical necessarily exists.

By modus ponens applied twice:

- R (Premise 1)
- R → ∃M_R (Premise 2) ⟹ ∃M_R
- ∃M_R → Physical (Premise 3) ⟹ Physical

## 3. Comparison with the Cartesian Cogito

|  | Cartesian Cogito | Naturalist Cogito |
|---|---|---|
| **Starting point** | "I am thinking" | "Reasoning is occurring" |
| **What is established** | That something exists | That something physical exists |
| **Substrate question** | Left open | Closed — substrate must be physical |
| **Dualism** | Compatible | Forecloses it |
| **Idealism** | Compatible | Forecloses it |

The shift from "I am thinking" to "reasoning is occurring" is deliberate. The first-person framing of the Cogito smuggles in a subject — an "I" — whose nature is then contested. The Naturalist Cogito strips the subject away and focuses on the act itself. Reasoning is happening. Whatever is doing it is secondary. What matters is that the act has requirements, and those requirements are physical.

## 4. Extension: The Thermodynamic Corollary

Once the substrate requirement is established, a further consequence follows from Landauer's Principle (1961): any logically irreversible operation on information — including the erasure or overwriting of a bit — dissipates a minimum of *kT* ln 2 joules of energy as heat.

Reasoning is a sequence of logically irreversible operations. Therefore:

**P3 (Extended):** Any physical substrate supporting reasoning necessarily generates entropy.

Formally: **∃M_R → dS(M_R, t)/dt > 0**

Combined with the core argument:

**R → dS/dt > 0**

To reason is to burn. Entropy generation is not an incidental side effect of thinking — it is a necessary consequence of it. This forecloses not only idealism but also the possibility of costless reasoning, reversible cognition, or thought without thermodynamic trace.

## 5. Implications

**Against solipsism:** Even if one doubts all external objects, the reasoning act that produces the doubt has substrate requirements. The substrate exists. Something beyond the pure doubting subject is necessarily real.

**Against idealism:** A purely mental universe in which no physical substrate exists cannot contain reasoning. The existence of reasoning is apodictic; therefore, a purely mental universe is not actual.

**For naturalism:** The argument derives physicalism from the inside — from the undeniable fact of cognition — rather than assuming it at the outset. It is a self-bootstrapping naturalism.

**For cognitive science and AI:** Any reasoning system — biological or artificial — is subject to these constraints. There is no substrate-independent cognition, and no cognition without thermodynamic cost. This has consequences for how we model minds, build AI systems, and evaluate claims about disembodied intelligence.

## 6. Conclusion

The Naturalist Cogito preserves the apodictic force of Descartes' starting point while extending it to a stronger conclusion: not merely that something exists, but that something physical necessarily exists, and that its existence is thermodynamically marked. Reasoning cannot be doubted; reasoning requires a physical substrate; physical substrates generate entropy. Therefore, to think is to burn, and to be is to be built.

> **R → dS/dt > 0**
